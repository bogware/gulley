# Gulley Out-Enforces Rivals, Lacks Agent-Era Breadth

Measured against twenty-plus LLM/AI gateways and the platform-native controls that Anthropic, OpenAI, AWS, Microsoft, Google, GitHub and Cursor now ship themselves, Gulley holds **six enforcement properties that no competitor documents as of 10 October 2026** — atomic reserve/commit budget admission, a hash-chained, KMS-signed, WORM-mirrored and externally anchored audit trail, a reversible tokenization vault that detokenizes the _response_, in-stream windowed enforcement on both Anthropic Messages and OpenAI chat/Responses streams, fail-closed residency/ZDR routing with a served-region stamp, and an Apache-2.0 core with nothing paywalled — but it **fails the 2026 RFP on breadth**. The field has moved from "LLM gateway" to "agent gateway": Gartner's May 2026 market overview lists **MCP gateway support as mandatory**, roughly twenty of the vendors surveyed ship MCP proxying with OAuth brokering and per-tool authorization, and Gulley ships none. Every competitor scopes budgets and rate limits to key, user, team and organization while Gulley stops at workspace, model and attribution; every serious competitor can capture redacted prompt/response content while Gulley's tamper-evident log is metadata-only; service accounts, end-user identity propagation, `count_tokens`, batches, images/audio/realtime and Gemini-native support are all absent. The 2026 consolidation wave (Portkey→Palo Alto Networks as Prisma AIRS AI Gateway, OpenRouter→Stripe, Helicone→Mintlify, Lunar→Boomi, Plano→DigitalOcean, Natoma→Snowflake, agentgateway and Envoy AI Gateway→Linux Foundation AAIF) means buyers increasingly meet a gateway bundled inside a security, data, payments or cloud platform, and the first-party gateways (Claude apps gateway, Databricks Unity Gateway `ug`, Azure APIM, AWS AgentCore) now give the basic tier away free. Pricing norms are stable: hosted routers take 5–5.5% of spend or price per recorded log, self-hosted vendors gate SSO/SCIM, audit, orgs, per-key budgets, secret managers and VPC/air-gap behind enterprise licenses — exactly the layer Gulley includes. The strategic read is that Gulley's wedge is real and verifiable but narrow; closing the top five gaps (MCP/A2A governance, principal-scoped budgets, opt-in redacted content capture into the chain, service/agent identities with token exchange, and API-surface completeness incl. `count_tokens` and Gemini) converts a differentiated niche into an RFP-passable product.

The MCP gap deserves the most attention because it is simultaneously the most widespread, the most RFP-weighted and the most hazardous to build. LiteLLM's MCP gateway is the broadest open-source reference (fixed `/mcp` endpoint, twelve auth modes including RFC 8693 token exchange and Okta ID-JAG, a six-level permission intersection where "most restrictive wins", tool pinning that freezes names/descriptions/schemas and alerts on drift, per-tool `allowed_params`, per-server and per-tool cost) ([LiteLLM MCP control](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/mcp_control), [LiteLLM MCP](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/mcp)) — and it has also been the most CVE-prone subsystem of 2026: CVE-2026-42271 (stdio command injection) chained with Starlette CVE-2026-48710 into an unauthenticated CVSS-10.0 RCE, and CVE-2026-59822 (OAuth2 passthrough auth bypass) landed in CISA KEV on 2 September 2026 ([Horizon3](https://horizon3.ai/attack-research/vulnerabilities/cve-2026-42271-chained-with-cve-2026-48710/), [LiteLLM advisories](https://github.com/BerriAI/litellm/security/advisories)). The maintainers themselves concede "five parallel credential paths plus an override cascade with no single resolver" ([LiteLLM issue #30484](https://github.com/BerriAI/litellm/issues/30484)). agentgateway is the cleaner design reference: virtual MCP federation with `prefixMode`, CEL `matchExpressions` that hide unauthorized tools from `tools/list` and return JSON-RPC `-32603` on denied calls, DCR brokering that serves `/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource/mcp`, SPIFFE workload identities since 1.5, and support for the stateless 2026-07-28 spec revision ([agentgateway tool access](https://agentgateway.dev/docs/kubernetes/latest/documentation/mcp/tool-access.md), [agentgateway MCP auth](https://agentgateway.dev/docs/kubernetes/latest/documentation/mcp/auth/about.md), [agentgateway spec compatibility](https://agentgateway.dev/docs/kubernetes/latest/documentation/mcp/spec-compatibility.md)). Kong's enterprise MCP proxy shows the audit bar — "every access attempt, allowed or denied, is written to the plugin's audit log" — and also the trap of bolting MCP onto an LLM plugin chain: it cannot share a route with Kong's AI guardrails at all ([Kong AI MCP Proxy](https://developer.konghq.com/plugins/ai-mcp-proxy/)). Gulley's existing CEL governance of the model's tool-call _intent_ is a genuine head start, since Portkey's MCP guardrails are deterministic-only ("LLM-only checks such as PII detection... are not available for MCP") ([Portkey MCP guardrails](https://portkey.ai/docs/product/mcp-gateway/guardrails)), but intent governance without a tool-execution path is invisible to a buyer whose RFP line reads "MCP support: full/partial/none".

The budget-scope gap is the one most likely to embarrass Gulley in a proof of concept, because Gulley's _mechanism_ is the best in the field while its _scope_ is the narrowest. LiteLLM budgets exist at global, organization, team, team-member, internal user, virtual key (with concurrent `budget_limits` windows), customer/end-user, agent (per-session) and per-model-per-key levels, with "a request is blocked if any level on its path is over budget" ([LiteLLM users](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/proxy/users), [LiteLLM access control](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/proxy/access_control)); Vercel stacks team, project, API-key and user budgets and rejects with `402 quota_for_entity_exceeded` ([Vercel budgets](https://vercel.com/docs/ai-gateway/observability-and-spend/budgets)); Cloudflare's spend limits "split by value" on any metadata key including the Access-verified `cf.user_id`, with twenty rules per gateway and a choice of 429 or Dynamic-Route fallback ([Cloudflare spend limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/features/spend-limits/)); Bifrost enforces customer → team → virtual key → provider-config with calendar-aligned resets including quarterly ([Bifrost budgets](https://docs.getbifrost.ai/features/governance/budget-and-limits)); and Anthropic's own gateway caps spend per user, IdP group and organization as per-seat defaults ([Claude apps gateway spend limits](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-apps-gateway-spend-limits)). Rate limits follow the same pattern: LiteLLM applies `tpm_limit`/`rpm_limit`/`max_parallel_requests` per key, user, team, model and tag with a saturation-aware dynamic limiter ([LiteLLM dynamic rate limit](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/proxy/dynamic_rate_limit)), Databricks allows twenty limits per endpoint across users, service principals and groups ([Databricks configure page](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/ai-gateway/configure-ai-gateway-endpoints)), and Kong's policy-based limiter matches on consumer, consumer group, IP, header, path, model and provider ([Kong AI Rate Limiting Advanced](https://developer.konghq.com/plugins/ai-rate-limiting-advanced/)). Extending Gulley's reserve/commit ledger to virtual key, user, team and org scopes (with the same Lua atomicity) is the single highest-leverage change in this report: it keeps the only correct enforcement semantics in the market while matching the scope every buyer assumes.

The content-capture gap is subtler and cuts against Gulley's strongest row. Gulley's chain proves that the metadata record is complete and unaltered; what it cannot prove is _what was said_. Anthropic's admin guidance says plainly that "if you need request-level audit logging or to route traffic by data sensitivity, we recommend you place a gateway between developers and your provider" ([Claude Code admin setup](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/admin-setup)), while its own gateway "doesn't log or store prompt or completion content" ([Claude apps gateway](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-apps-gateway)); OpenAI's Compliance Logs retain thirty days and "aren't a complete audit trail for every file operation, shell command, browser interaction, tool call, or approval" ([OpenAI admin FAQ](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/enterprise/work-admin-faq)); GitHub's Copilot audit log "does not capture client-side prompts" [snippet] ([GitHub Copilot audit logs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/how-tos/administer-copilot/manage-for-enterprise/review-audit-logs)). That is the opening: nobody first-party stores content across Claude Code, Codex and custom apps and across Anthropic/Bedrock/Vertex/Foundry/OpenAI, and the vendors that do store it (Cloudflare, Portkey, Kong, LiteLLM, Databricks) have no tamper-evidence. Kong's audit schema even logs the _original_ PII next to the sanitized text with "no general redaction option documented" ([Kong AI audit log reference](https://developer.konghq.com/ai-gateway/ai-audit-log-reference/)). An opt-in, per-workspace content-capture mode that writes vault-tokenized bodies into the hash chain, with Portkey-style "metrics-only" as the default and Cloudflare-style per-request `collect-log-payload` override, would be unique.

Identity propagation is the gap that will grow fastest. The 2026 standard shape is an inbound IdP JWT bound to a user, exchanged per tool or upstream call under RFC 8693 or ID-JAG, with the identity stamped into audit and billing: TrueFoundry's three-layer model records "which user made each request, even when the upstream call uses shared credentials" ([TrueFoundry MCP auth](https://www.truefoundry.com/docs/ai-gateway/mcp-gateway-auth-security)), Bifrost's `token_exchange` swaps the caller's IdP token per call without persisting it ([Bifrost MCP auth](https://docs.getbifrost.ai/mcp/auth/overview)), agentgateway issues per-request short-lived JWTs and SPIFFE identities ([agentgateway releases](https://github.com/agentgateway/agentgateway/releases)), and Entra Agent ID / Okta for AI Agents went GA in May and April 2026 respectively ([Entra Agent ID](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/agent-id/whats-new-agent-id), [Okta release overview](https://www.okta.com/products/release-overview/)). Anthropic's Workload Identity Federation accepts "SPIFFE JWT-SVIDs" and issues short-lived `sk-ant-oat01-` tokens bound to service accounts via RFC 7523 ([Anthropic WIF](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/workload-identity-federation)); OpenAI's rollout guide says "issue one scoped credential per developer" and that Codex "doesn't support `apiKeyHelper`" ([Deploy Codex through a gateway](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/enterprise/roll-out-a-gateway)). Gulley's lack of service accounts is also shared by the Claude apps gateway ("a CI job with no developer to approve the sign-in can't authenticate") ([Claude apps gateway](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-apps-gateway)), which makes a gateway-issued service identity with WIF-style federation a differentiator against the first-party option as well as a parity item.

The surface and provider gaps are mechanical but real. Anthropic's compatibility contract lists `/v1/messages/count_tokens` as an optional forward and `GET /v1/models?limit=1000` (3-second timeout, ids containing `claude` or `anthropic`) for discovery ([Anthropic gateway compatibility](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/llm-gateway-protocol)); Agent Router added `count_tokens` and `/tokenize` in 1.1 ([Agent Router releases](https://github.com/envoyproxy/ai-gateway/releases)); Portkey added Claude Code `/v1/models` discovery in 2.22.0 ([Portkey enterprise changelog](https://portkey.ai/docs/changelog/enterprise)). LiteLLM bills Anthropic batches on completion and polls background Responses ([LiteLLM Anthropic passthrough](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/pass_through/anthropic_completion)); Gulley's reserve/commit model needs a documented policy for non-reservable batch spend. For Bedrock, AWS's own cost guide states that when "a gateway or proxy calls Amazon Bedrock on behalf of many users, Amazon Bedrock records the gateway's IAM role as the caller's identity" and prescribes per-user `RoleSessionName`/session tags plus request metadata ([AWS Bedrock cost management](https://docs.aws.eu/bedrock/latest/userguide/cost-management.html)); agentgateway implements this with fail-closed expressions so "no request reaches Bedrock unattributed" ([agentgateway attribution](https://agentgateway.dev/docs/kubernetes/latest/documentation/llm/cost-controls/attribution.md)). Gulley's API-key-only Bedrock path cannot do this today.

## Six controls no competitor matches as of October 2026

The differentiators below are stated with the specific competitor evidence that bounds them. Each is a property a buyer can verify in a proof of concept rather than a marketing claim.

**Reserve/commit budget admission that is TOCTOU-safe and meters partial spend on abort or failover.** Every vendor except LiteLLM documents overshoot as expected behavior (quoted in the matrix discussion above), including Cloudflare ("spend is eventually consistent so bursts can briefly exceed") ([Cloudflare spend limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/features/spend-limits/)), MLflow ("the crossing request completes") ([MLflow budgets](https://mlflow.org/docs/latest/genai/governance/ai-gateway/budget-alerts-limits/)), Apigee ("the last allowed request might consume more tokens than the remaining quota") [snippet] ([Apigee LLMTokenQuota](https://docs.cloud.google.com/apigee/docs/api-platform/reference/policies/llm-token-quota-policy)) and Bifrost's worked example where the request is allowed and "the next request will be blocked" ([Bifrost budgets](https://docs.getbifrost.ai/features/governance/budget-and-limits)). Portkey's budget engine syncs Redis "every 10 seconds", carried a "Redacted due to a budget enforcement bug" release note and a March 2026 double-counting fix ([Portkey enterprise changelog](https://portkey.ai/docs/changelog/enterprise), [Portkey changelog March 2026](https://portkey.ai/docs/changelog)). LiteLLM's reservation exists but is bounded by fail-open defaults, a 2026 bug list including "Budget enforcement bypassed in v1.82.3" (#26672) and "Cross-end-user budget leak" (#29142) ([LiteLLM issues](https://github.com/BerriAI/litellm/issues)), and the open P0 above. Gulley's differentiator is therefore "the only gateway whose documented budget semantics are reserve-worst-case-then-commit-actual with atomic Redis Lua and a single teardown that commits before best-effort sinks", not "the only gateway with reservation". It is also the right time to say so: Anthropic frames its own caps as "a circuit breaker rather than an invoice" ([Claude apps gateway spend limits](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-apps-gateway-spend-limits)) and Traefik calls its costs "an estimate... not an invoice" ([Traefik token rate limit](https://doc.traefik.io/traefik-hub/ai-gateway/middlewares/token-rate-limit)); invoice-grade enforcement is unclaimed ground.

**Hash-chained, KMS-signed, externally anchored, WORM-mirrored and offline-verifiable audit.** The nearest competitor is Traefik Hub's Transparency Logs — a licensed add-on that hashes log _lines_ into a Tessera Merkle tree with signed checkpoints and optional witness cosigning, "stores only IDs and hashes, not content", excludes bodies, and depends on external witnesses for split-view protection ([Traefik Transparency Logs](https://doc.traefik.io/traefik-hub/api-gateway/observability/transparency-logs)). Bifrost offers HMAC per entry on _administrative_ events only, delegates immutability to operator-configured S3 Object Lock, and "the word 'tamper' is never used" in its docs ([Bifrost audit logs](https://docs.getbifrost.ai/enterprise/audit-logs)); LiteLLM's audit logs cover key/team/user/model mutations with "no tamper-evidence or hash-chaining claims" ([LiteLLM multiple admins](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/proxy/multiple_admins)); Portkey's are admin-action-only with no chain ([Portkey audit logs](https://portkey.ai/docs/product/enterprise-offering/audit-logs)); Kong's MCP/A2A "audit" is plain structured logging; F5 and Tetrate claim "audit trail" without a mechanism ([F5 product page](https://www.f5.com/products/ai-gateway), [docs.tetrate.ai](https://docs.tetrate.ai/)). OpenAI describes its Compliance Logs as "immutable, append-only" but retains them thirty days [snippet] ([OpenAI compliance API help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9261474-compliance-api-for-chatgpt-enterprise-edu-and-chatgpt-for-teachers)). Gulley's DB-trigger-plus-REVOKE chain with asymmetric KMS signing, external anchoring and an offline evidence bundle is unmatched on mechanism; the caveat — content-less — is the gap in the previous section.

**Reversible tokenization vault with response-side detokenization, KMS envelope and BYOK crypto-shred.** Kong's AI PII Sanitizer is the only competitor with any restoration, and its docs limit it: "restoration applies only to input sanitization", it requires a ≥600 MB spaCy sidecar, streaming is not mentioned, and the audit log carries the originals ([Kong AI PII Sanitizer](https://developer.konghq.com/plugins/ai-sanitizer/), [Kong AI audit log reference](https://developer.konghq.com/ai-gateway/ai-audit-log-reference/)). Bifrost's `runtime_reversible` mode produces numbered placeholders "scoped to Bifrost logs, not external destinations", with reveal gated to `Logs:Reveal` holders and "Reveal is not supported" in plain runtime mode — it never detokenizes the model's response for the caller ([Bifrost redaction](https://docs.getbifrost.ai/enterprise/guardrails/redaction)). Traefik's Content Guard states "Reversibility: Not addressed" ([Traefik Content Guard](https://doc.traefik.io/traefik-hub/ai-gateway/middlewares/content-guard)); agentgateway's regex masks are one-way with masking disabled on streams ([agentgateway guardrails](https://agentgateway.dev/docs/kubernetes/latest/documentation/llm/guardrails/overview.md)); Google's Model Armor can de-identify/tokenize via Sensitive Data Protection but the Gemini Enterprise integration "doesn't support de-identification or masking" ([Model Armor integrations](https://docs.cloud.google.com/model-armor/integrations)); Bedrock Guardrails "doesn't support the masking of sensitive information with asynchronous mode" ([Bedrock Guardrails streaming](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-streaming.html)); Anthropic inference hooks are "allow or deny. Rewriting or redacting a prompt is not supported" ([Anthropic inference hooks](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/inference-hooks)). The one engineering caveat is Anthropic's compatibility contract: "a gateway that rewrites or redacts request bodies for content inspection breaks the pairing the same way stripping does, so inspect without modifying", and preserved-thinking signatures reject when `system`, `tools` or earlier `messages` differ from the request that produced them ([Anthropic gateway compatibility](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/llm-gateway-protocol)). Gulley's masking must therefore be deterministic for a conversation prefix; if it already is, that determinism should be documented as a feature, because it is the property that lets reversible masking coexist with Claude Code.

**In-stream windowed enforcement (redact/mask/block) on Anthropic Messages and OpenAI chat/Responses streams.** Streaming output control is the weakest row across the field: agentgateway's response guards are off on `stream: true` by default and "Mask on streams: Does not apply... Matching content passes through unmodified" ([agentgateway guardrails](https://agentgateway.dev/docs/kubernetes/latest/documentation/llm/guardrails/overview.md)); Traefik's response rules "buffer the full completion" ([Traefik Content Guard](https://doc.traefik.io/traefik-hub/ai-gateway/middlewares/content-guard)); Kong's semantic response guard needs a `response_buffer_size` ([Kong AI Gateway changelog](https://developer.konghq.com/ai-gateway/changelog/)); Bifrost holds "the full stream... until evaluation finishes" when any rule can block ([Bifrost guardrails](https://docs.getbifrost.ai/enterprise/guardrails)); Portkey's streaming output guardrails arrived only in 2.5.1 (April 2026) as "a final chunk added to the stream" while its overview still says "on streaming, output guardrails are informational only" ([Portkey changelog April 2026](https://portkey.ai/docs/changelog/2026/april), [Portkey guardrails](https://portkey.ai/docs/product/guardrails)); MLflow's post-LLM guardrails "are not triggered for streaming requests" and Databricks states "output guardrails are not supported for... streaming" ([MLflow guardrails](https://mlflow.org/docs/latest/genai/governance/ai-gateway/guardrails/), [Databricks configure page](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/ai-gateway/configure-ai-gateway-endpoints)); Cloudflare DLP buffers "the full streamed response" when response scanning is on ([Cloudflare DLP](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/features/dlp/)). The closest competitor is LiteLLM, whose v1.104.0 (3 October 2026) added "Mask PII in streaming /v1/messages output" while its Responses-API guardrails still "apply only to non-streaming requests" ([LiteLLM v1.104.0 notes](https://docs.litellm.ai/release_notes/v1.104.0/v1-104-0), [LiteLLM Responses API](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/response_api)). Gulley covering Messages _and_ chat _and_ Responses in-stream is unique; the credibility move is to publish p99 added latency with the enforcer on, since competitors now argue latency (Lakera "<50 ms", Lasso LEAP "under five milliseconds", Palo Alto "sub-millisecond routing" — all vendor claims) ([Check Point AI security](https://www.checkpoint.com/ai-security/), [Lasso LEAP](https://www.lasso.security/resources/lasso-security-announces-future-of-ai-security-with-cpu-based-guardrails), [PANW GA blog](https://paloaltonetworks.com/blog/2026/07/announcing-general-availability-of-prisma-airs-ai-gateway)).

**Fail-closed data-residency/ZDR routing with a served-region stamp in the audit record.** The hosted peers come closest: Vercel's `inferenceRegion` fails with HTTP 400 rather than cross-region fallback and reports the resolved `geoRegion` per provider attempt, with a published per-provider ZDR table and the footnote that `anthropic/claude-fable-5` has no ZDR provider ([Vercel regional inference](https://vercel.com/docs/ai-gateway/security-and-compliance/regional-inference), [Vercel ZDR](https://vercel.com/docs/ai-gateway/security-and-compliance/zdr)); OpenRouter's `provider.zdr` restricts to ZDR endpoints but OpenRouter "does not change routing based on provider retention policies" otherwise ([OpenRouter provider routing](https://openrouter.ai/docs/features/provider-routing), [OpenRouter privacy](https://openrouter.ai/docs/features/privacy-and-logging)); Cloudflare's ZDR "does not apply to BYOK requests" ([Cloudflare Unified Billing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/features/unified-billing/)). Among self-hosted gateways none documents residency-aware routing beyond LiteLLM's `region_name` pre-call filter ([LiteLLM routing](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/routing)) and Portkey has "no regional endpoints or data-residency statements" and has retired air-gapped deployment ([Portkey gateway URLs](https://portkey.ai/docs/product/enterprise-offering/org-management/gateway-urls), [Portkey feature comparison](https://portkey.ai/docs/product/product-feature-comparison)). Gulley's combination — fail-closed, stamped into a tamper-evident record, self-hosted and air-gap-capable — is unique, and it aligns with Anthropic's `inference_geo` (US-only at 1.1× price) and ZDR constraints ("Claude Fable 5.1... require 30-day data retention and are not available under ZDR unless expressly authorized") that a gateway must surface rather than hide ([Anthropic data residency](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/data-residency), [Anthropic API and data retention](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention)).

**Everything in the Apache-2.0 core, including SSO, SCIM, audit, secret-manager integration and residency.** The open-core line is drawn in the same place by every vendor: LiteLLM gates SSO beyond five users, SAML, SCIM, JWT auth, audit logs, organizations, secret managers, tag/model budgets, log export and air-gap behind `LITELLM_LICENSE` ([LiteLLM enterprise](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/enterprise)); Kong's AI Proxy Advanced, rate limiting, semantic cache, PII sanitizer, MCP proxy, MCP OAuth2 and A2A proxy each state they are "only available as part of our AI Gateway Enterprise offering", and AI Gateway 2.0 removed the free `ai-proxy` plugin entirely ([Kong AI Gateway changelog](https://developer.konghq.com/ai-gateway/changelog/)); Traefik's AI and MCP gateways are "sold as add-ons to Traefik Hub API Gateway" ([Traefik pricing](https://traefik.io/pricing)); Bifrost's guardrails, cluster mode, SSO, Vault integrations, audit logs and RBAC are Enterprise ([Bifrost pricing](https://www.getmaxim.ai/bifrost/pricing)); Portkey's OSS column is "✗ logs, traces, config management, LLM key management, caching, prompts, guardrails, RBAC, audit logs, SSO" and its public repo has no 2.x tag despite the March 2026 "fully open source" announcement ([Portkey feature comparison](https://portkey.ai/docs/product/product-feature-comparison), [Portkey GitHub tags](https://github.com/Portkey-AI/gateway/tags)); TrueFoundry's audit logs and data residency are Enterprise-only ([TrueFoundry pricing](https://www.truefoundry.com/pricing)). Only agentgateway and Agent Router are fully Apache-2.0 — and neither has USD budgets in core, a UI, or SSO (Solo and Tetrate sell those) ([docs.solo.io](https://docs.solo.io/agentgateway/latest/), [docs.tetrate.ai](https://docs.tetrate.ai/)). Gulley is the only gateway where the regulated-buyer feature set is in the free tier.

Three further properties are strong but not unique. Gulley's raw-byte fidelity with pre-first-byte-only failover matches Anthropic's contract ("if your gateway buffers a response until it is complete, Claude Code stalls") where LiteLLM deliberately walks "every entry of a fallback list after a mid-stream failure" and agentgateway 1.6's new default translates Messages into OpenAI Responses and "drops extended-thinking history" ([Anthropic gateway compatibility](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/llm-gateway-protocol), [LiteLLM v1.104.0 notes](https://docs.litellm.ai/release_notes/v1.104.0/v1-104-0), [agentgateway releases](https://github.com/agentgateway/agentgateway/releases)) — but Agent Router's `streamIdleTimeout` also fails over "before the first token" ([Agent Router releases](https://github.com/envoyproxy/ai-gateway/releases)). Shadow-spend reconciliation against the Anthropic and OpenAI admin cost APIs has no documented counterpart in the notes (Vercel's `gateway.cost` vs `gateway.marketCost` is the nearest cousin), but absence of evidence is not proof of absence. The `gulley` CLI acting as Claude Code `apiKeyHelper` and Codex auth command with refresh-reuse detection is matched in spirit by the Claude apps gateway `/login` device flow (Claude-only, one OIDC issuer) and Databricks `ug`, and in UX by `vercel ai-gateway setup` for 29 agents ([Claude apps gateway](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-apps-gateway), [Databricks `ug` CLI](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/ai-gateway/coding-agent-ug-cli), [Vercel coding agents](https://vercel.com/docs/ai-gateway/coding-agents)); the dual Claude-Code-plus-Codex coverage is the distinguishing part, and OpenAI's statement that a Claude `/v1/messages` gateway "doesn't prove Codex compatibility" turns dual-native support into a checklist item ([OpenAI gateway compatibility](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/enterprise/gateway-compatibility)).

What this implies for Gulley is fourfold. The set of vendor-neutral, self-hostable, actively maintained gateways with governance in core is now very short — LiteLLM (MIT core, enterprise gating, security record), Bifrost (Apache-2.0 core, enterprise gating), TrueFoundry (seat-priced, hybrid), MLflow (free, minimal), and the two AAIF projects (Apache-2.0, no budgets/UI in core) — so "not bundled, not paywalled, not a security vendor's sidecar" is a scarce position rather than a default one. Second, buyers will increasingly compare Gulley against a _platform_ they already own (Prisma AIRS in a PANW shop, Unity Gateway in a Databricks shop, APIM in Azure, AgentCore on AWS, the Claude apps gateway in a Claude-only shop), so the pitch must be cross-platform parity — one policy and one audit chain across Claude Code, Codex, Bedrock, Vertex, Foundry and OpenAI — rather than feature-for-feature. Third, acquisitions freeze OSS lines (Portkey, Helicone) and shift roadmaps toward the acquirer's packaging, which is a credible argument for an independent Apache-2.0 project but only if Gulley's own release cadence is visible. Fourth, the AAIF projects set the design vocabulary (Gateway API CRDs, CEL policy, SPIFFE, MCP 2026-07-28); matching their concepts — CEL expressions over `jwt.*`/`mcp.*`/`llm.*`, token exchange, virtual MCP servers — lowers evaluation friction with platform teams who already run Envoy.

The 2026 _Hype Cycle for AI Governance Technologies_ (relayed by TrueFoundry) places AI gateways at "1% to 5%" penetration and "Embryonic" maturity, expects "consolidation into a single offering that supports multiple use cases", and lists adjacent categories "FinOps for agentic AI, AI usage control", "agentic AI security" and "AI agent action rollback (Embryonic)" ([TrueFoundry Hype Cycle blog](https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/9-takeaways-from-gartner-r-2026-hype-cycle-tm-for-ai-governance-technologies)). Gartner's September 2025 _Innovation Insight: MCP Gateways_ named TrueFoundry and Enkrypt AI as representative providers ([Enkrypt](https://www.enkryptai.com/blog/gartner-innovation-insight-mcp-gateways-2025)); Noma is a "Market Shaper" in the 2026 Emerging Market Quadrant for AI Application Security and Pillar a 2026 Cool Vendor ([noma.security](https://noma.security/), [Pillar blog](https://www.pillar.security/blog/pillar-security-named-as-a-2026-gartner-r-cool-vendor-in-ai-software-security)).

The buyer checklist that emerges from the vendor guides and the platforms' own admin documentation is consistent enough to treat as the de facto RFP. Aklivity's tiering [vendor opinion] calls provider abstraction, routing, retries, fallback, rate limits, token accounting, telemetry, MCP proxying, session/streaming handling, auth, quotas, cost allocation and audit logging "becoming baseline"; semantic routing, MCP federation, tool-level authorization, OAuth mediation, enterprise identity, data-loss controls, content safety and compliance records "advanced today"; and policy over tool arguments and side effects, agent-to-agent governance, delegated authority narrowing, "approval as runtime state" and outcome-tied observability "emerging differentiation" ([Aklivity](https://www.aklivity.io/state-of-ai-gateways-2026)). Zuplo's buyer's guide [vendor-authored] enumerates provider coverage (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google baseline plus Azure OpenAI, Bedrock, Mistral, Cohere, self-hosted), MCP graded full/partial/none, routing/fallback on 429, semantic caching, guardrails (prompt injection, PII, content policy), token-level rate limits per user/app/window, "spend caps and budgets (daily, weekly, monthly)", "hierarchical budgets from organization to team to application", "real-time cost attribution per request, consumer, and model", key issuance/rotation/revocation, key vaulting, OAuth/JWT with IdP, TTFT, "prompt and response logging with configurable PII redaction", session/trace support for multi-step agents, SOC 2/HIPAA/FedRAMP, data residency and deployment model ([Zuplo buyer's guide](https://zuplo.com/learning-center/best-ai-gateway-buyers-guide)). Anthropic's own admin page sets expectations for coding-agent buyers: usage monitoring via OTel, an analytics dashboard, programmatic per-user reporting, spend controls, ZDR, HIPAA, "request-level audit logging" and routing "by data sensitivity" ([Claude Code admin setup](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/admin-setup)). Against this list, Gulley passes routing, caching, guardrails, key vaulting, OIDC/SCIM, audit and residency; fails MCP, hierarchical budgets, per-user limits, content logging, FedRAMP/HIPAA posture and service accounts; and has unverified status on `/v1/models` discovery and multi-step trace correlation.

Four 2026 themes reshape what "table stakes" means. The **agent-gateway redefinition**: MCP 2026-07-28 made MCP stateless (no `initialize`/`Mcp-Session-Id`), added `server/discover` and `Mcp-Method`/`Mcp-Name` headers, and aligned authorization with OAuth 2.0/OIDC through six SEPs ([AWS ML blog on MCP 2026-07-28](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/how-agentcore-gateway-supports-the-mcp-2026-07-28-spec/)); the MCP "Enterprise-Managed Authorization" extension built on ID-JAG was promoted to stable on 18 June 2026 per secondary sources, with Claude, Claude Code and Cowork reported as clients ([WorkOS ID-JAG explainer](https://workos.com/blog/mcp-enterprise-managed-authorization-id-jag)). **Agent identity** converged on SPIFFE for workload identity plus RFC 8693 token exchange for delegation, with Anthropic's WIF accepting "SPIFFE JWT-SVIDs" and Red Hat's "permission intersection" rule that agents can only reduce user permissions ([Anthropic WIF](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/workload-identity-federation), [Red Hat Next](https://next.redhat.com/2026/06/10/wiring-zero-trust-identity-for-ai-agents-spiffe-token-exchange-and-kagenti/)). **FinOps**: FOCUS 1.4 was ratified on 4 June 2026 without token or model columns — "native token tracking, model identity and a Price Sheet dataset are on the FOCUS 1.5 roadmap, targeted for around December 2026" — while the State of FinOps 2026 reports "98% of FinOps teams now manage AI spend... up from 31% two years ago" and the FinOps Foundation notes a "lack of generally accepted frameworks for cost allocation across multi-agent workloads" ([Flexera FinOps X recap](https://www.flexera.com/blog/perspectives/finops-x-2026-recap/), [FinOps for AI overview](https://www.finops.org/wg/finops-for-ai-overview/)). That gap is Gulley's: FOCUS will not carry per-user or per-agent attribution, so the gateway is the only place it can be produced, and a FOCUS-shaped export with per-agent/per-prompt rows (fed by the `x-claude-code-agent-id`/`-prompt-id` headers Gulley currently ignores) is a durable FinOps wedge. **EU AI Act**: Article 12 requires high-risk systems to "technically allow automatic recording of events (logs) over the system's lifetime"; the Digital Omnibus (provisional deal 7 May 2026) moved Annex III obligations to 2 December 2027 and Annex I to 2 August 2028, with most Article 50 transparency duties still starting 2 August 2026 ([EU AI Act Article 12](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/12/), [JD Supra on the Omnibus](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/eu-agrees-digital-omnibus-deal-to-4275721/)). A tamper-evident per-request log with identity is the obvious evidence artifact — but the obligation sits with providers and deployers of high-risk systems, so Gulley should position the chain as evidence tooling, not compliance.

Two compliance facts constrain positioning. Any gateway, first- or third-party, forfeits Anthropic's HIPAA configuration ("an LLM gateway or any other custom `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL`: No") and CMEK does not cover Claude Code telemetry behind a gateway ([Claude Code HIPAA setup](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hipaa-setup), [Claude Code enterprise deployment](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/third-party-integrations)); Gulley must bring its own BAA story, as Cursor and Portkey do ([Cursor enterprise](https://cursor.com/docs/enterprise), [Portkey pricing](https://portkey.ai/pricing)). And Anthropic "doesn't endorse, maintain, or audit third-party gateway products", attaches "has not been audited for security" to the LiteLLM reference, and offers a managed pin — `allowedProviders: ["customEndpoint"]` plus `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` — so that "Claude Code then refuses a session pointed anywhere else" ([Claude Code other LLM gateways](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/llm-gateway), [Claude Code manage costs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs)); Gulley's onboarding packs should emit exactly that pin, and its security posture (fail-closed keys, hash chain, distroless signed image) should be presented explicitly as the answer to the "unaudited" caveat.

## Governance is the paid layer everywhere except Gulley

Pricing follows three models and one consistent paywall. Hosted routers charge a percentage of spend or per recorded request: OpenRouter takes 5.5% ($0.80 minimum) on credit purchases, charges nothing on BYOK up to $25,000 per month and 5% above, and gives a 1% discount for opting into prompt logging ([OpenRouter FAQ](https://openrouter.ai/docs/faq)); Vercel adds "zero markup" on tokens including BYOK but bills surcharges for governance — custom reporting at $0.075 per 1,000 writes plus $5 per 1,000 queries, team-wide provider allowlist $0.10 per 1,000 requests, team-wide ZDR $0.10 per 1,000 requests, trace drains $0.05 per 1,000 traces plus $0.50 per GB ([Vercel pricing](https://vercel.com/docs/ai-gateway/pricing)); Cloudflare's core is free on all plans with a 5% fee on Unified Billing credits, Legacy Logs capped at 100,000 total on Workers Free, Logpush at $0.05 per million after 10M, guardrails billed as Workers AI inference and DLP limited to two profiles without Zero Trust ([Cloudflare Unified Billing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/features/unified-billing/), [Cloudflare pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/reference/pricing/)); Requesty takes a 5% markup with "all features included" ([Requesty](https://www.requesty.ai/)); Helicone charges $0/$79/$799/custom by log volume and retention ([Helicone pricing](https://www.helicone.ai/pricing)). Per-log pricing is the second model: Portkey's Developer tier is free for 10,000 recorded logs with three-day retention and "not suitable for production workloads", Production is $49 per month for 100,000 logs plus $9 per additional 100,000, and Enterprise starts at 10M+ ([Portkey pricing](https://portkey.ai/pricing)) — a competitor estimates "$2,000–$10,000+/mo" for the enterprise tier and cites Kong at "$100 per model per month, capped at 5 models on the Plus tier" [competitor-authored] ([TrueFoundry Portkey pricing guide](https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/portkey-pricing-guide)). Seat-plus-request pricing is the third: TrueFoundry charges $0 (3 users, 10K requests/user/month), $25 per user per month (20K pooled, 10 guardrail policies, 10 budget rules, 30-day retention, SSO and custom roles) and custom Enterprise, with $20 per additional 100K requests and "requests and MCP tool calls count equally" ([TrueFoundry pricing](https://www.truefoundry.com/pricing)). Self-hosted open-core vendors sell licenses sized to deployment: LiteLLM Enterprise is "sized to your annual gateway request capacity, deployment architecture, and support needs", never per token, with a 30-day trial and marketplace procurement ([LiteLLM pricing](https://www.litellm.ai/pricing)); Bifrost and Solo are custom with 14- and 30-day trials ([Bifrost pricing](https://www.getmaxim.ai/bifrost/pricing), [Solo licensing](https://docs.solo.io/agentgateway/kubernetes/latest/documentation/install/licensing/)); Prisma AIRS requires "a Prisma AIRS license with flex credits" ([Prisma AIRS AI Gateway docs](https://docs.paloaltonetworks.com/prisma-airs/ai-gateway/ai-gateway-overview)).

The paywall line is drawn in the same place by every self-hosted vendor: identity (SSO beyond a handful of users, SAML, SCIM, JWT auth), tenancy (organizations, custom roles, RBAC), compliance (audit logs, log export, data-lake export, retention controls, air-gap/VPC), advanced money controls (per-key, per-tag, per-model budgets, spend reports, temporary increases), secret-manager integration, and SLAs — plus, in Kong and Portkey, semantic caching and most guardrails, and in Kong and Bifrost, the MCP proxy or federated MCP auth ([LiteLLM enterprise](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/enterprise), [Portkey feature comparison](https://portkey.ai/docs/product/product-feature-comparison), [Kong AI Gateway changelog](https://developer.konghq.com/ai-gateway/changelog/), [Bifrost pricing](https://www.getmaxim.ai/bifrost/pricing)). Databricks charges for payload logging and usage tracking but not for rate limits, fallbacks or traffic splitting [snippet] ([Databricks AI Gateway GCP](https://docs.databricks.com/gcp/ai-gateway)); Anthropic's gateway ships free inside the `claude` binary and its Claude Code Analytics API "is free to use", while SCIM and the Compliance API require Claude Enterprise ([Claude apps gateway](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-apps-gateway), [Claude Code Analytics API](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/claude-code-analytics-api), [Claude Code feature availability](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/feature-availability)); OpenAI's Business plan is $20–25 per user with SAML, and SCIM, Compliance API and residency are Enterprise ([ChatGPT & Codex pricing](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/pricing)). Two consequences follow. The platforms have compressed the value of the _basic_ free tier — OIDC sign-in, model allowlists, per-user caps and OTLP telemetry for a single vendor's traffic are now free from Anthropic, Databricks, Microsoft and AWS — so an all-free gateway cannot win on "free" alone. But the _governance_ tier that every independent vendor charges for is precisely what Gulley includes, which supports a pricing posture of free software with paid support, SLAs, hardened builds (FIPS) and hosted control-plane options, mirroring how Tetrate and Solo monetize Apache-2.0 projects without gating features.

## Conclusion

The research reframes Gulley's position from "an enterprise LLM gateway with some unusual features" to "the only gateway whose enforcement semantics are correct, bolted to a surface that is a year behind the category". The correctness properties — admission-time reservation, chained and signed audit, response-side detokenization, in-stream enforcement across both canonical wire formats, fail-closed residency with provenance — are not merely absent in competitors; most competitors _document the opposite behavior_ as accepted, which makes Gulley's claims cheap to prove in a bake-off and hard for incumbents to retrofit without rewriting their data planes. What has changed is the definition of the category: with Gartner calling MCP mandatory, the AAIF projects setting the CEL/SPIFFE/token-exchange vocabulary, the platforms giving away single-vendor basics, and the identity vendors making "agent = first-class principal" the norm, a gateway that governs model calls but not tool calls, and workspaces but not people, services or agents, reads as incomplete regardless of how sound its ledger is.

The implication is a sequencing decision rather than a repositioning. The highest-leverage moves reuse Gulley's existing primitives: extend the reserve/commit ledger from workspace to key/user/team/org scopes; extend the CEL tool-intent engine into an MCP proxy with per-tool authorization, pinning and token exchange; extend the hash chain to carry opt-in, vault-tokenized content; extend the virtual-key broker to issue service and agent identities via WIF-style federation; and extend attribution to consume the Claude Code headers and the Bedrock session-tag recipe so the FinOps export carries rows FOCUS never will. Done in that order, each step converts a documented competitor weakness into a Gulley feature that is both RFP-visible and verifiably unique, and the resulting product is the one the consolidation wave has made scarce — vendor-neutral, fully open, and correct by construction.
